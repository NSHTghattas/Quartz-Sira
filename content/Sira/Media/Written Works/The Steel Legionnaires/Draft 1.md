# The Synopsis
A crack team of Steel Legionnaires are sent to deliver a weapon to the forces stationed at the Sapphire Isles. But they encounter many obstacles along the way, including but not limited to; an old age ironkin destroyer, the sea, and undead. And most dangerously, themselves.

There are five people. Here are their names:

- Kinujitev, Steel Leader of the lot, specialized with a spear that can be recalled via runed glove. Moderately armored. Has a shield. One of the more calculating of the lot, but still hungers for a good fight. (Male Orc)
- Zuvistov, Heavy Weapon Expert. A warhammer for when in a melee, an oversized crossbow with a harpoon attached, made feasible courtesy of thaumatophysical treatment. Heavily armored, tough to beat up. Very jolly. No shield for this big one. (Male Human)
- Ulovistin, Crackshot. With a crossbow and a khopesh, this soldier specializes in one thing and one thing only: Deadly accuracy. In your average combat, an archer might get off 10 shots if they are lucky. Ulv only shoots three. Difference is, each of those arrows find their mark every single time. Has a quiver of regular arrows, and a quiver of special arrows, each enchanted for maximum use. Also jolly. (Female Human)
- Vuvitsky, Combat Medic. Vuvistsky is able to dole out punishment, and in turn lighten the others' as well. Kit consists of: A medpack, a sling, a scimitar, and some healing runes. Probably the squishiest of them all (or maybe not?), but what this soldier lacks in fire-power more than makes up for in unflinching support, willingness to obey orders, and patching up fellow teammates. Silent type, but is actually the rookie of the group. (Male Human)
- Sudogostin, War Vibrologist. Let's balance out all the green stuff, shall we? Learned in the discipline of War Vibrology (thousands of Brassic snobs wince at this), Sudo can unleash fire and destruction upon his/her foes. Of course for that to work Sudo needs a bell and a wonderful singing voice. Which he/she has. Awesome. The one most prone to brashness and hot-headed behavior, of course. (Male Orc)


Now for what you have been waiting for.

Here is their story...

# Part 1, The Tundra
## Chapter 1
### Vuvitsky's log, entry 1

 The weather is bloody cold. The mission objective is bloody far away. And whoever decided to give us this *Oredenno-forsaken mission can bloody well die*.

I feel the cold weather of the Steel tundra bite through my furs, causing me to shiver, as we travel through mountainous terrain. The others seem to be taking the cold in stride, as if it was business as usual, and knowing them, it probably was. Coming from a warm climate does not help matters in the slightest, either. Right, where was I? Ah, yes. The Mission.

This is my first entry for the log, for the purposes of...being sentimental, maybe? I do not know, but it feels right to write anyway. With me are four other soldiers, each sharing a bond that can only be forged in battle, which coincidentally they see no end of. Nor do they intend to. I still don't quite know their names, to my embarrassment, but I at least know the name of the leader of the squad I recently find myself in. Kinujitev, one of the only two Orcs here. I only hope I can match his expectations, as this is the first outing where I participate in-

===

The silent medic abruptly stops writing as soon as he noticed the gaze of the wider man of the group, Zuvistov. Zuvistov chuckles heartily, amused at the way Vuvitsky freezes like children getting caught doing something they are not supposed to.

"What are you writing in there, rookie?" He asks the medic.

"Nothing of consequence." He replies after a moment. The reedy man really does not like to talk, does he? Well then, Zuv shall find something else to occupy the little man's head. After a moment, he breaks the silence.

"Nice weather we are having here, da?" 

The rookie thinks for a moment, then nods slowly.

*Hah, he hates it!*

"I am sure the furs are impeding your enjoyment of this weather," Zuv continues, then gestures to his comparatively light clothing, "You can be like me, take off those furs. Here, give them to me and let yourself enjoy the *true* eastern experience!" He mostly meant that as a joke, as well as a half-baked hazing attempt.

To his disbelief, Vuvitsky actually takes off his furs, leaving only the armor underneath, after some hesitation, and hands them to Zuvistov. He could hardly believe his eyes!

*Let's see how long he lasts...*


===
### Vuvitsky's log, entry 1, continued

Why? Why, oh why, did I do this? Am I stupid? Airheaded? I take off my furs (which were barely protecting me from the severe weather) and just *gave* them to the man who needs it least, the stocky one. He looked so happy though, and I couldn't just say *no*, could I?

I check my mobile cycle-watch. It is almost time to rest, and I can see a cave opening nearby. Perhaps it is time we should rest. I bring it up with the others, seeing how they react. They don't seem perturbed by my suggestion of an early night, which is miles better than when I suggested going through the river (they laughed all the way until we reached these mountain ranges, by the way. How was I to know that the river was freezing cold?). I will end this log here, and pick up the quill again when something interesting happens.

===

The silent medic puts away his writing implements, and musters the courage to get his furs back. After some laughing, he finally can rest.

## Chapter 2
Science has many definitions. Some could say that Science is the study of pushing the limits of different aspects of life. Per this definition, some might say I am a human scientist. 

But then again, some might say that pushing the limits of how much I can embarrass the new rookie does *not* qualify as a science. To them, I say 'Bold words to say within crossbow range'.

Whether a science or not, Vuvitsky is as red as a tomato, and I think I may have achieved a new record. Zuvistov certainly thinks so, if his laughter is anything to judge by. Anyway, we are currently on our way out of the big mountain range, after which shortly the port. I am excited for the ship journey, despite popular opinion of it's general nature. If the jaws of Death dares to latch on to me, it has a crossbow full of trouble to worry about! I pat my crossbow reassuringly, making sure I didn't accidentally forget a weapon I use so much I dream of it before we set off two weeks ago.

You know, normal people stuff.

"Ulov," Begins Kinujitev, beleaguered, "please stop doing that. The day you actually forget your crossbow is the day you take up the rapier."

"Sir, you would earn my eternal enmity." I deadpan, though I internally shudder at the prospect of having to use a *rapier* of all things. No need for such dark thoughts now.

A round of laughter follows, though only chuckles come from the Rookie, as he was still recovering from my light teasing. Or he is just quiet. Quite boring, I would say.

We were laughing so hard that we almost didn't notice the corpse in front of us. It was Vuvitsky, as nervous and paranoid as he was, that noticed it first.

"Corpse!" He called out, silencing our gales of laughter and instantly injecting adrenaline into the atmosphere. With a practiced speed, a history of several past confrontations guiding my hand, I instantly have my crossbow out, already loaded for times like these. Some crossbow experts say that always having a bolt loaded puts too much stress on the string, and should generally be avoided. But replacing a string is always easier that not having the crossbow bolt when you need it.

Everyone else was similarly ready, now we were all wary as we slowly approached on foot (we weren't on horses) to the corpse. Zuvistov got his warhammer and tentatively got a closer look. Then he chuckles. "It's a Tinman! buried so deep in the snow, too. Must have been dead for quite some time, yes?"

Relief spread through most of our features, but Kinujitev remained concerned. "Do not touch it, it could be a destroyer."

Silence reigns. A destroyer? this is dire news, if true. Destroyers are Ironkin of the long past, back when we were at all out war with them. These tinmen sacrificed their sanity and the chance at a normal life, in exchange they got monstrous powers through augmenting themselves purely for war. This was not your run-of-the-mill Ironkin soldier who got a Crossbow Arm, or an Armblade. The Destroyers deviated so much from the standard Ironkin that even among their own kind they were regarded as monsters, a necessary evil, so to say. Multiple wicked arms, no hands but only sharp blades and arrows that can pierce stone. Legs that can traverse rocky terrain with ease, and climb unnaturally steep places. Malevolent gazes that burned holes through wherever they looked. One is a match for three hardened Steel Legionnaire squads of the past. We are only one hardened squad by modern standards. The worst thing about these Destroyers is that they like to play dead, and wait for large masses of soldiers to pass by before they emerged from the ground and just slaughtered the squads. When the war was over, a great many of Steel veterans couldn't take ten steps without breaking into a cold sweat.

Quite understandably, a few of us swore loudly. Then were immediately shushed, lest we woke up the sleeping monster. We slowly moved around the very-possibly-not-dead Destroyer, and continued on our merry way, trying our hardest to pretend all was well and we didn't just have a near death experience.

"You owe me a drink, after this." Zuvistov rightfully stated, having been the closest to the body, and was further cemented by nods from all.

"Whatever you want, Zuv." Kinujitev replied.

As the wary Steel Squad slowly started releasing their tension while approaching the end of the mountain route, a metallic finger twitched.

## Chapter 3

Sudogostin, the second of the two Orcs in the squad, inspected his sling, making sure there were no faults in it. Of course he disdained in such mundane weaponry, but if there was a possibility that he had to contend with ancient Bronze crafts, such as that Destroyer they had passed a while back, then he needed to consider that his Talent might not come into play here. Until now he dearly wished to see how he could overpower the Vibrologic immunity all those Bronze equipment had, but as far as he could tell it was as natural as fire against water. Thaumatophysics and Vibrology simply don't mix, and not even in a fun 'explode in your face' kind of way, but in a 'pathetic canceling each other out' kind of way. Anyways, he finished inspecting the sling and was moving on to the dagger when their Most Glorious Commander Kinujitev spotted enemy hostiles in the distance.

Finally, some action.

---
The aforementioned Most Glorious Commander, Kinujitev, had spotted some errant undead. Much like the ones they would be facing at their destination. He mind goes back to the pack he is wearing, contained in it a package not to be opened by anyone but the recipient. Who happens to be in the Sapphire Isles.

The undead group seems to be of the tougher variants, indicating that at least one of them was the stronger type, those who could almost think. Certainly smart enough for leadership and tactics. Bad for anyone else, but manageable for those who hail from the tough lands of the Steel Kingdom. Ones such as themselves.

"Incoming undead." Kinu intoned to the rest of the team, getting their attention. "I want everyone ready, I will distract the crowd with Zuv, I want Ulovistin on the biggest, meanest one. Sudogostin you will be wrecking their side of the fight while trying not to scorch us, Vuvitsky you are on standby if Sudo does turn out to hit one of us. Otherwise fire at will."

Those orders were given rapid fire, giving each man (or woman in Ulov's case) a clear goal. Their formation quickly changed, with Zuvistov coming ahead next to Kinujitev, Ulov going to the left, and the medic staying with Sudo to the right of the formation. Eventually they got near enough for the rest of the squad to see what Kinu had spotted. There was a Ghoul leading a group of 9 skeletons and zombies, the lighter weight dead being at the back. Already a bad sign, if the more fragile ones are taking care of their self preservation instincts.

Now any other man would suggest lying in wait for the undead to come by before ambushing them. There *is* a certain allure to that idea, Kinujitev admitted, but stealth was not really their area of expertise. Or even a rudimentary skill. Ulovistin might pull it off, but this wasn't a group of Ulovistins, so Kinu was of a mind to just advance into their ranks, the front two drawing any potential fire that would come.

So he did just that.

---
Sudogostin was now getting his war bell out and holding the hammer in it. He didn't want to attract the notice of the undead just yet. Also Kinujitev would be furious and find some way to discipline him. He look to his right, finding the new medic nervously checking over his scimitar and medical pack, both. *'About time we got a medic'*, Sudo thought to himself, *'I was getting real tired of using my energy on healing injuries. What's the point in a War Vibrologist who doesn't fight?'*

"First time fighting undead?" The Orc asks.

Vuvitsky looks up. "Hmm? Oh yes." He goes back to attending to his weapon, almost winds up tripping (never a good idea to closely inspect the edge of a blade while walking blindly), then gives his full attention to Sudo. "Do you have advice?"

"Easy, aim for the head. That's where all the undead power comes from supposedly, meaning they are just as susceptible to beheadings as-"

"I know how to kill undead." The rookie interrupts (rather rudely, in Sudo's opinion), "I mean what am I supposed to do when one comes at my face when I don't have a weapon to behead it with?"

Sudo ponders this for a moment. Then shrugs. "That's the thing. If you don't have your weapon with you, you might as well lay down and die."

"How reassuring."

"Don't mention it."

"Quiet, the both of you." Kinujitev tells them off from ahead. "We are about to engage."

And then the undead noticed them and charged, and the fight began.

## Chapter 4
The two forces clashed in the snowy valley of the Steel Tundra, the deep snow hampering the movement from an all out run (generally not an advisable thing to do in a regular circumstance, but leave it to Steelies to disregard common sense) to a sort of awkward march. Fortunately the enemy were affected in a similar way, so it all even out. When they were nearly at melee range, Ulov stopped moving and began sighting on her crossbow, aiming for the Ghoul leader of the lot, trusting Sudogostin to take care of the archers for her.

Meanwhile Sudogostin began chanting, a sound not far too dissimilar with the hymns for Oredenno back at the temples. As he worked with his music, Kinujitev and Zuvistov finally closed in on the undead combatants, and with a war cry Kinu speared the nearest zombie through it's decaying head, stopping it dead in it's tracks, while Zuvistov batted at another zombie with a war hammer, taking it's head clean off in a way that was decidedly less graceful. Kinu saw the opening created by Zuv's hammer swing, and dashed in to shove the tide of the (considerable lighter armored) undead backward, fouling the shots the few archers at the back near the ghoul were aiming. Zuvistov followed by taking a wide swing at the horde, stepping in to increase his momentum, and aiming for disabling more undead rather than focusing a lethal blow on a single one. 

Bones broke and flesh tore as three undead stumbled back, all of them moving even slower now that their skeletal structure obstructed more fluid movement. Kinujitev, taking advantage of the second opening left by Zuv, quickly made there lightning fast jabs at the enemies, putting down three more. In mere seconds, the duo's clear-and-strike tactics netted them five casualties, but the initial headstart was running out. There was only so much two legionnaires, even ones such as themselves, could do against fourteen combatants alone. And then Sudogostin's bell finally clanged.

With the first strike of his bell, Sudogostin proved his worth to the squad. As soon as he had struck the bell, Sudogostin's eyes turned golden, another golden glow suffusing his bell. He mimed throwing the bell at the part of the horde about to encircle the duo from the right, retaining his bell, but launching the glow suffusing the bell.

Before anyone could catch sight of it, a good third of the remaining undead horde caught on fire and were charred to ashes before they could put it out in the snow. The resonating echo of the clang resonated with the special Thaumatophysical equipment each legionnaire except Sudo himself had, but did nothing worse to it. The Ghoul saw that it was clearly on the losing side, and garbled some inhuman noise, probably a signal to retreat, but it had not finished it's sentence before a crossbow bolt shot through the mouth and exploded through the back of the skull. With the Ghoul collapsing in the snow, bleeding only a little, so too did the tactical strength of the remaining six undead collapse. What was previously an organized mob, archers at the back infantry front, now devolved into a scramble to get the closest legionnaire. 

Ulovistin put away her crossbow, satisfied that she had done her part in the fight. Meanwhile Kinujitev and Zuvistov cleaned up the remaining undead with the same clear-and-strike tactic they had previously employed. All the while Vuvitsky had been watching in awe, having not participated much in the fight in favor of watching Sudo's back. Seeing that there were no more hostiles, everyone calmed down from the adrenaline of the fight. Then a cheer rose.

"Let it be known," The Orc leader announced, "That this is the best fighting force I have been in command of in the entirety of the Steel Kingdom!" A few cheers, falsely modest comments, and a teasing comment replied to the leader. "Really, I would not trade any of you for all the wealth in the world. Except maybe Sudogostin, who could bore a Bronze scholar!" Now laughs spread at that statement, including Sudogostin himself participating in good humor.

"Alright, enough dallying. We have a package to deliver. But first we need to bury the bodies."

A few of the members groaned, namely Ulovistin and Sudogostin. "But Kinu," Ulov spoke up, "why can't we just leave it for the next group to come by? Don't you always say that it's a joy to see one's enemies buried beneath their feet? Let's give the next group that joy!" 

"I agree, Leader." Sudogostin threw in his support, seeing himself as above such things as making the path more convenient for others to pass through. That was for the non-artistic types after all.

"Alternatively," Kinujitev spoke, "the one who buries the most bodies gets a drink on me next tavern stop."

To say that the squad, even Vuvitsky included, buried the bodies with competitive glee would be an understatement.

## Chapter 5
In the wee hours of the Second Cycle, also more colloquially known as "Orc Cycle", the newest member of the team found himself unable to sleep. He tossed and turned in his sleeping bag, his mind replaying that day's events over and over, analyzing each detail. The way the squad had held themselves, the way Kinujitev seemed to convey so much information in so few words and phrases, the flawless coordination between him and Zuvistov, and so much more. Vuvitsky could not hold it much longer. He had to write, to chronicle this.

Slowly and quietly, as to not wake the others, the medic opened his pack and retrieved his chronicle, as well as a quill and ink, and began to write under the light of the ever present Sun.

## Chapter 6
It is a most beautiful day to be awake. Granted, every moment of the day looks the same, whether Human or Orc Cycle, but Zuvistov found it prudent to enjoy any moment of the day you are not dead or asleep. Of course, the moments he enjoyed most was fighting, the thing that could very well end his life any day. But what is a life that is lived without risks? Risks are what makes you appreciate everything that much more.

And then Zuvistov awoke from his partially groggy, and inexplicably philosophical state, and took a long pull from his waterskin. Mmm, tastes like an animal bladder. That's how you know your waterskin is good. Or was it the other way?

Dismissing that thought for future-Zuvistov, the big man looked around the camp site, seeing that only he is awake. As is the usual. With a grunt of exertion, he heaves himself up to his feet, bends backwards to relieve his back muscles with a satisfactory *pop*, then goes about making breakfast. Of course, this being the tundra full of snow that it is, breakfast did not usually entail lighting a campfire and cooking eggs over it. No, it was simpler than that. He simply took some syrupy liquid from a wooden container he had in his large pack (the largest one of everyone's aside from Kinu, who's delivery is quite large), kept warm by some green runes engraved into the wood. 

Digging out some shallow, hand-sized pits in the snow, Zuv then took out the world-famous Ration Bars, the peak of military cuisine, though at the bottom of pretty much any other cuisine, for a reason that did not surprise Zuvistov. Nobody knows what goes into the making of such a bar, and most would not want to. All this to say, there is a reason Zuv brought out his warm syrup liquid thing. Something called "Maple" or other.

The infantry man pours the syrup sparingly over the five holes he had dug in the snow, filling them up to the brim. Leaving the liquid to rapidly cool, Zuvistov put the bars gently on each of them, before wrapping the now solid Maple syrup around the ration bar, creating something new. Something innovative.

A slightly less disgusting ration bar.

The promise of such new food was carried with it's aroma, making it's way through the cold wind and into the nostrils of each sleeping member, encouraging them to wake gently from their dreams. Sudogostin awoke as Zuvistov finished skewered the last of the bars and putting away the rest of his equipment.

"Zuv? What's this?"

Looking up from his pack, the big man smiles, then replies,

"Breakfast. Here, take this one." He holds out the maple-covered ration bar by the skewer, bar facing Sudo.

Taking it, the Vibrologist raises a brow. "Some ambitious new food doomed to fail again, I take it?"

"Not as bad as your cooking." Without a second's hesitation, Zuvistov claps back.

"At least I don't see ration bars as viable options."

"You would have us prepare you five courses, like you were some noble. I bet you don't even know what's in those bars."

"Oh? And why should I inflict that type of harm to myself? Do you wish me damaged, Zuvistov? I thought friendly fire was below you."

"The only thing below me is your intelligence."

At that point Ulov announced her awakening by throwing a pillow at Zuvistov, interrupting their friendly argument. "Shut your mouths, I am trying to sleep."

At this, both men laugh. Resigned to her fate of not ever sleeping again from the two noisiest laughers of the team, Ulovistin gets up. Then looks at 'breakfast'.

"Zuv? Please don't tell me we are meant to eat those."

---
After a somewhat unfulfilling breakfast, the group began moving again to their destination. They were reaching the final stretch of their first journey to the docks, sticking to the path paved for them by previous travelers through the forest., but struggling through snow nonetheless. Already there was snowfall again, and the wind did not get any warmer for Vuvitsky's tastes. He did not 'dress like an authentic Easterner' again Zuvistov tried to get his furs again. Zuv concedes that it probably would not be as funny the second time, anyway.

But something *does* seem odd with the new medic. He didn't get enough sleep clearly, judging by his red eyes. Also perhaps his muttering might be a clue. Whatever it was, it probably traced back to his little book. Zuvistov might ask the man if he could read it sometimes, he is curious about what Vuvitsky writes in that thing all day. Probably how amazing Zuvistov was. Or how handsome he was. Or how he excelled at both aspects at once.

A side effect of this lack of sleep that was most telling, however, was his jumpiness. He kept swearing that he saw something move in between the trees, then reassuring everyone that it was probably nothing, or maybe small wildlife. The first time it happened, everyone laughed it off in good humor, with even Ulov concocting a theory of how their mightiest enemies might not even be the Brass, but only the Squirrels of Doom that roamed the lands. The second time it happened everyone chuckled. 'Why so jumpy, Vuvistky?' was asked on numerous occasions. It was when it happened a _third_ time that Zuvistov began to think that it might not just be Vuvistky's immense paranoia that was speaking.

Kinujitev was apparently of the same mind, as he gave out an order. "Weapons up."

Some looks between the group were had, but everyone listened regardless. You do not disobey your squad leader in a time like this. Better for the rookie to be wrong and never let it go for eternity than to be caught with their pants down figuratively. (Although Zuvistov did remember a situation where that was literal. That was not a pleasant bathroom break.) Ulovistin loaded her crossbow, Kinujitev strapped on his shield before taking out a spear, Zuv got his warhammer, Sudogostin his bell. And then Vuvitsky brought out a scimitar.

"You fight with a scimitar, medic?" Sudo commented. "Nice."

This statement was echoed by Zuvistov and Ulov, all briefly admiring Vuv's weapon in solidarity. Of course, to that he awkwardly nodded, and said thanks under his breath. 

After that short exchange, the group moved as a unit, maintaining awareness of their surroundings, ready to defend themselves at a moment's notice.

Unfortunately for them, what struck was by far faster.

It was Ulovistin who first noticed a faint illumination at the back of the group, through the trees. It took a split second for her to recognize it as fire. And that it was moving towards them. Quickly.

"Get down!"

With instincts honed from many years ago, back at basic training, and driven into them by demanding trainers and live fire alike, they listened to Ulov's warning and hit the ground fast. The fireball sailed over them and hit the trees, engulfing them in a flame, the yellow and orange of a regular flame. Tinted green.

Kinujitev was the first to rise, and began to pick up his spear as he looked wildly around the area where the fireball came. The others began to rise as well. There didn't seem to be a second attack forthcoming, but there was now no way they would continue walking without a care in the world. Not with their adrenaline so high. Not with the possibility that they were being hunted. By what exactly was still a mystery, but one that must be solved soon, with the end of a spear.

"Formation three! Encircle Sudo!" Immediately the squad's training kicked in, and they began to spread out, maintaining a circle around Sudogostin, who stood at the center of the circle, ready to attack anyone who came close to the team.

Now that they were on very high alert, they could hear faint clicking footsteps. Like someone was wearing very heavy armor. Or three people.

Or an Ironkin Destroyer.

A metallic blur suddenly burst out from the forest from another side than were it had thrown the fireball, jumping at Zuvistov. With a cry, the big warrior was instantly thrown down, desperately trying to get the metal thing off of him. With each second he didn't get it off, he collected an impressive set of scratches, scars and injuries. What stood over Zuvistov gave most of everyone pause.

The rust colored Destroyer had a serpentine form, head ending in a maw like the dragonkin. It stood on the ground with four wickedly sharp claws, with an extra set of them mauling at the infantry man. It's tail ended in a blunted hammer, trying to find a good angle with which to strike with. It's throat glowed with the light of almost molten metal, radiating sweltering heat and melting the show around it's head. It had ruins of what appeared to be wings on it's back, with only a framework remaining.

It was an imitation of the dragon. More importantly it was a mockery of *Oredenno* himself. To make a mockery of The Dragon himself was more than enough to snap the team out of their daze. With the coldness of a soldier deeply angered, Ulovistin was the first to break the trance. She discarded her mundane bolt, replaced it with one that was glowing with green runes, took aim, then fired at the thing.

The Destroyer noticed the speeding bolt and made to scamper away, but it was too slow. Fortunately for it, it had managed to get it's head out of the way of the bolt. The bad news was that the bolt had sped up exponentially, and grown to the size of a small tree. It skewered itself through the Destroyer, dragging it with the bolt in it's course. The path terminated at a tree behind it, effectively pinning it helplessly to the tree.

This triggered another round of staring, though only on Vuvistky's part this time, before Kinu yelled, "Move!" and they began to run at an all out sprint through the last leg of their journey.

For your information Field Arcanist Gapius has taken explicit permission from Legionnaire Vuvitsky for assistance in some project or another, and cannot be overruled by Steel Legion authority."
